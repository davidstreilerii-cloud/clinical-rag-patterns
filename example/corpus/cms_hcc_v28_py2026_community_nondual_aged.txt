CMS-HCC VERSION 28 RISK-ADJUSTMENT COEFFICIENTS -- PAYMENT YEAR 2026
SEGMENT: COMMUNITY NON-DUAL AGED (CNA)

Scope note: national model coefficients. No beneficiary, plan or provider is
involved. A coefficient is the additive contribution of that HCC to the raw risk
score for a beneficiary in this segment.

The nine segments in the V28 model are: Community Non-Dual Aged, Community
Non-Dual Disabled, Community Full Benefit Dual Aged, Community Full Benefit Dual
Disabled, Community Partial Benefit Dual Aged, Community Partial Benefit Dual
Disabled, Institutional, New Enrollee, and C-SNP New Enrollee. Coefficients differ
between segments; a coefficient quoted without its segment is not interpretable.

V28 renumbered the HCCs relative to V24, and the numbers do NOT carry over. In
V28 the diabetes categories are HCC36 / HCC37 / HCC38 and the heart failure
categories are HCC222 through HCC226. Checked against this file: there is no
payment HCC 85 in V28 at all, and HCC111 does exist but is Hemophilia/Male --
a number surviving into V28 attached to an entirely different condition is more
dangerous than a number that simply disappeared.

How to read the coefficient magnitudes, computed from the rows above:
  * Largest three: HCC49 Specified Lysosomal Storage Disorders at 9.256,
    HCC111 Hemophilia/Male at 4.639, HCC17 metastatic cancer to lung, liver,
    brain and other organs at 4.209. The top two are rare conditions with very
    high drug costs, not common ones.
  * Smallest: HCC328 Chronic Kidney Disease Moderate (Stage 3B) at 0.127.
  * The three diabetes categories all carry 0.166. In this model, documenting a
    chronic complication does not by itself raise the diabetes contribution,
    though it may map the encounter to additional distinct HCCs and to the
    diabetes-heart-failure interaction term (not reproduced here).
  * HCC224, HCC225 and HCC226 all carry 0.360; the separation in heart failure
    payment sits at HCC222 End-Stage and HCC223 assist device, both 2.505.

A coefficient near 0.2 is a routine chronic condition. A coefficient above 4 is
rare and expensive. Nothing in this segment sits between 4.639 and 9.256.
