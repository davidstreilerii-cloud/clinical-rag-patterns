A Standardised Infection Ratio (SIR) is observed infections divided by the number
predicted from the NHSN baseline for the same case mix. Above 1 is worse than
predicted, below 1 is better.

IMPORTANT, and the reason this short table is worth including:

OBSERVED FACT: CMS publishes the national value as a bare '1' for all six
measures, with no decimal places. The JSON API and the CSV distribution agree,
so this is the published precision and not a transport artefact. No more precise
national figure is available from this source.

LIKELY EXPLANATION, stated as inference and NOT as something this source says:
an SIR is normalised against a baseline, so a national aggregate landing at the
reference point of 1 is what you would expect. This corpus does not carry the
NHSN documentation that would confirm it, and no attempt was made here to derive
the value independently.

Either way, the operational consequence holds: do not quote a spread of national
SIR values across infection types from this table. It does not contain one.
Facility-level and state-level SIRs do vary and are published elsewhere.
